Dissertations and internships

Every year a handful of students spend a few months to a year in the lab on a dissertation, a training programme or an internship, and several have ended up as authors on papers. This page sets out the routes in, what the projects look like, and how to write to us.

Routes into the lab

Pick the one that matches where you are. If none of them fit, write anyway and say so.

BS-MS or M.Sc. dissertation
A six to twelve month thesis project, usually the final year of a BS-MS or M.Sc. programme. This is the most common route and the one that most often leads to a publication. Come with your institute's paperwork and timeline. 6–12 months · year-round
CSIR-CCMB Dissertation Research Training Programme
CCMB's own scheme for students carrying out dissertation research here, with a stipend. Applications go through CCMB; write to us as well, so we know to look out for your file. 6–12 months · CCMB scheme
CCMB Summer Training Programme
A short summer project for undergraduate and Masters students, advertised by CCMB early in the year. Competitive, and a good way to find out whether this kind of work suits you. 2–3 months · summer
Short research internship
A few weeks to a few months, self-arranged, usually unfunded unless you bring a fellowship. Best for learning one specific thing well: a bioinformatics pipeline, a library preparation, a morphological technique. 4 weeks and up · year-round
Visiting student from another lab
If you are doing a Ph.D. elsewhere and need a technique, a probe set or a dataset that we have, a short visit is often the fastest route. Several people have done exactly this. by arrangement

Projects we could start tomorrow

Real, self-contained pieces of the lab's work. Examples rather than a menu — the shape of any project depends on how long you have and what you want to learn.

Genome erosion in mites

Compare gene family content across acarine genomes and ask which losses are shared and which are lineage-specific. Almost entirely computational.

Comparative genomicsPython or R

Ultraconserved elements for a neglected order

Take an order with few sequenced representatives, capture UCEs from museum material, and put the first tree on it. Wet lab plus phylogenetics.

Where has the redback actually reached?

Mine SpiderIndia and iNaturalist records for the introduced Australian redback, model its potential range, and compare it with native widow spiders.

Does macrosynteny agree with sequences?

Test chromosome-level gene arrangements against sequence-based trees for a group of your choice, and quantify where the two disagree.

Work through museum and freshly collected material, describe what is new, and produce an identification key. Microscopy, illustration and fieldwork.

What to expect

A real question, not a task list

You will be told what is unknown, what we suspect the answer might be, and why it matters. Then you go and find out.

You will learn to compute

Almost everything here touches the command line at some point. No prior experience is expected; a willingness to be bad at it for a few weeks is.

Authorship where it is earned

Students who do the work go on the paper. Several names in our recent publications first arrived here as students.

Weekly meetings

One-to-one time each week, plus lab meeting. If something is not working we would rather hear about it in week two than in month four.

Fieldwork, sometimes

Depending on the season and the project you may end up in the Eastern Ghats or the Northeast. It is not guaranteed, and it is not compulsory.

Honest limits

We usually cannot pay for short internships, and Hyderabad accommodation is your own arrangement unless a CCMB scheme covers it. Ask early rather than late.

How to write to us

One email, to siddharth@csirccmb.org. It does not need to be long, but it does need to be specific.

Subject line
The route you are applying for and your dates — for example, BS-MS dissertation, Jan–Jun 2027. Generic subject lines get lost.
What interests you
A paragraph on which part of the lab's work you want to be near, and why. Naming one of our papers and saying what you would do next with it works far better than praise.
What you can already do
Programming, molecular lab work, microscopy, fieldwork, statistics, illustration, languages. Say honestly what you have not done as well; nobody expects all of it.
Attachments
A CV of one or two pages, and your transcript if your programme requires it. One PDF is easier than five.
Timing
Write three to six months ahead. Summer places fill by February or March, and dissertation slots by the middle of the preceding semester.
If we do not reply
Send one polite reminder after ten days. It is almost always an overloaded inbox rather than a decision.
